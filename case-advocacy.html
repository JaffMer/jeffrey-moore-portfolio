Project Analysis

Arts Advocacy & Community Outreach Campaign

Transforming a classroom research unit into a stakeholder-facing communication campaign about the benefits of music.

Stakeholder Communication · Knowledge Transfer
← Return to Portfolio

Design Challenge

How do you help learners understand the value of what they are learning while also preparing them to explain that value to someone else?

Some learners questioned the relevance of music education, so the challenge was to move beyond simply telling them why music mattered. Learners needed to research evidence-supported benefits themselves, make sense of what they found, and communicate those ideas clearly to a broader audience.

AudienceLearners, families, community stakeholders
TimelineUnit-based project
RoleLearning designer & facilitator
DeliverablesResearch task, rubric, job aid, peer review, survey

Solution

What I Designed

I designed a research-based project where learners identified evidence-supported claims, synthesized their findings, created brochures for a real audience, used peer feedback to improve their work, and gathered community responses through a QR-code survey.

Design decisions

The Choices Behind the Work

Authentic Audience

The brochures were not created only for a grade. Finished work was shared with an actual audience, giving learners a reason to think carefully about clarity, accuracy, visual design, and whether their message would make sense to someone outside the classroom.

Evidence Before Advocacy

Before learners could advocate for an idea, they needed evidence they could stand behind. Research activities helped them identify key claims, locate supporting sources, and decide which information was strong enough to communicate to others.

Just-In-Time Support

A Canva job aid addressed a recurring technical barrier and allowed learners to complete their deliverables more independently. The technology supported the learning goal rather than becoming the barrier to it.

Results & impact

What Changed

  • Learners moved from questioning the value of music to researching and communicating evidence-supported benefits themselves.
  • Finished brochures included QR codes that invited feedback from the audience.
  • The outreach activity generated 9 community responses.
  • Peer review and public feedback gave learners a stronger sense of audience, purpose, and accountability for the quality of their work.

Design Reflection

The strongest design decision was taking the project beyond a classroom assignment. Giving learners a real audience changed the purpose of the work. They were not researching information just to prove they could find it. They had to decide what mattered, support their claims, communicate clearly, respond to feedback, and create something another person could actually learn from. That shift made the learning more meaningful because the work had a purpose beyond completing the assignment.
