Design Challenge

How do you prepare a high-performing employee to lead others when the skills that made them successful as an individual contributor are not necessarily the same skills they will need as a manager?

The challenge was to help first-time managers redefine their role, establish professional boundaries, adapt their leadership approach, and build the confidence to coach and develop employees effectively.

AudienceNewly promoted and first-time managers
Estimated TimeApproximately 40–45 minutes
RoleInstructional designer & eLearning developer
DeliverablesRise course, scenarios, readiness check, manager toolkit

Design Approach

Learning through Marcus's promotion journey.

Marcus, first introduced as a new technician in the onboarding experience, returns after earning a promotion. I used his transition to give learners realistic management decisions to work through, opportunities to see the consequences of different choices, and situations that connect leadership concepts to challenges they may encounter during their first months in the role.

Learning Experience

What learners develop.

Manager and Leader Responsibilities

Learners distinguish operational management from people leadership, then discover through scenario practice that strong managers intentionally combine both.

Dynamic Leadership

Six leadership approaches are applied to business situations before learners explore Dynamic Leadership: adapting behaviors to the people, context, and desired outcome.

Coaching and Development

Managers practice timely feedback, professional boundaries, the SBI framework, and development planning supported by downloadable on-the-job resources.

Manager Toolkit

Performance support beyond the course.

I believe manager development should continue long after formal training ends. As part of the continuous improvement plan, I created practical resources managers can use before coaching conversations, during employee development, and when following up on performance over time.

Design Reflection

My goal was to treat first-time manager training as the beginning of development, not the end of it. Scenario practice gives learners a safe place to work through difficult leadership decisions, while the SBI tools and Evergreen Growth Plan carry that learning into real conversations and continued employee development. Together, the course and performance support resources create opportunities for practice, application, follow-up, and continuous improvement.
