Project Analysis

Digital Media Production Pathway

Building digital literacy, collaboration, creativity, and project management through scaffolded podcast development.

Digital Learning · Project-Based Learning
← Return to Portfolio

Design Challenge

How do you help learners become confident with a complex digital platform without overwhelming them at the beginning?

My initial approach gave learners freedom to explore Soundtrap, but access to the technology was not enough. Without clear guidance, foundational skills, and a structured path forward, engagement and the quality of learner output were inconsistent.

AudienceGeneral music learners
TimelineApproximately 3 weeks
RoleLearning designer & facilitator
DeliverablesGuided tutorial, planning guide, rubric, peer review

Solution

What I Designed

I redesigned the experience into a scaffolded three-week pathway. Learners moved from foundational digital music concepts and guided platform exploration into remix practice, podcast planning, collaborative production, milestone check-ins, peer review, and final reflection.

Design decisions

The Choices Behind the Work

Build Complexity Gradually

Learners built confidence through increasingly complex tasks before being asked to create a complete podcast. Each step introduced new skills while reinforcing what they had already practiced.

Collaboration With Purpose

Learners worked in pairs and could interview one another or an outside subject matter expert. Collaboration created opportunities to divide responsibilities, communicate expectations, solve problems together, and manage a shared project.

Peer Review & Reflection

Learners evaluated peer work against shared standards while reflecting on the quality of their own project. This gave them another source of feedback before final submission and encouraged revision rather than treating the first attempt as finished.

Results & impact

What Changed

  • Learners became more confident navigating Soundtrap and moved from guided practice toward independently creating collaborative digital projects.
  • Project milestones helped learners manage pacing and kept larger tasks from becoming overwhelming.
  • Clear rubrics gave learners a stronger understanding of expectations and a reference point for evaluating their work.
  • Peer review created an opportunity to receive feedback, revise, and improve quality before final submission.

Design Reflection

This project reinforced for me that giving learners access to a tool is not the same as teaching them how to use it successfully. My initial approach gave learners too much independence before they had the confidence or foundational skills to use Soundtrap effectively. Building a structured pathway with guided practice, checkpoints, feedback, and gradually increasing independence gave learners the support they needed before asking them to create on their own. The goal was not to remove independence. It was to build learners toward it.
