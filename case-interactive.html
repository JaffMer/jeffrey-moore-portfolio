Project Analysis

Interactive Portfolio Experience

Using personalization and curiosity to increase engagement with portfolio content while collecting feedback for continuous improvement.

Engagement · UX Design · Evaluation
← Return to Portfolio

Design Challenge

How do you make a professional portfolio something recruiters and hiring managers actually want to explore?

I did not want visitors to simply scroll through another static collection of work samples. The challenge was to create an experience that encouraged interaction, demonstrated personalization and branching, and gave visitors a more memorable way to learn about me and my work.

Try Interactive Experience
AudienceRecruiters, hiring managers, portfolio visitors
TimelinePrototype and ongoing iteration
RoleLearning experience designer
DeliverablesBranching activity, learner profiles, analytics, feedback form

Solution

What I Designed

I designed an interactive experience that uses branching logic to respond differently based on a visitor's choices. After answering a series of questions, visitors are directed to a personalized learner profile before moving into a scenario, reflection, and optional portfolio feedback. The experience gave me a practical way to demonstrate branching and personalization while making the portfolio itself more engaging.

Design decisions

The Choices Behind the Work

Curiosity As Motivation

The activity begins with self-discovery, giving users a personally relevant reason to participate.

Reflection After Results

Participants connect their profile to prior learning experiences, turning the activity into a reflective learning moment.

Evaluation Loop

Feedback and analytics were built into the experience to support ongoing UX improvements.

Results & impact

What Changed

  • 91 active users were recorded during the initial evaluation window.
  • Forms and analytics provided an initial source of visitor feedback and behavior data.
  • Several instructional design professionals later reviewed the portfolio in detail and shared qualitative feedback through LinkedIn.
  • That feedback directly informed revisions to project framing, mobile usability, interaction cues, navigation, and the voice used throughout the site.

Design Reflection

This project reinforced that engagement does not end when something is published. My original vision included a live activity dashboard that I ultimately did not develop, but the larger feedback loop proved more valuable. Watching how visitors interacted with the portfolio and listening to detailed feedback from other instructional designers helped me identify what was working and what needed to change. The portfolio has continued to evolve because of that feedback.
