Project Analysis

Rehearsal to Concert: A Performance-Based Learning Experience

A scaffolded learning experience that moved learners from foundational skill development through practice, feedback, and coaching toward confident application in a high-stakes live performance.

Learning Transfer · Assessment · Performance Support
← Return to Portfolio

Design Challenge

How do you prepare learners with different skill levels to confidently apply what they have learned when the final assessment is a live performance?

Learners could often demonstrate individual skills during rehearsal, but consistently applying those skills in an authentic performance required intentional scaffolding, ongoing assessment, individualized coaching, and opportunities to practice before the stakes were real.

AudienceMiddle school choir learners
TimelineSemester-long concert cycle
RoleLearning designer, facilitator, coach
DeliverablesRubrics, forms, practice tracks, reflection tools

Solution

What I Designed

I designed a performance-based learning experience that connected scaffolded rehearsal, formative assessment, practice resources, real-time coaching, authentic performance, and post-performance reflection. Each part of the process informed the next, with the concert serving as the final demonstration of learning rather than the only learning event.

Design decisions

The Choices Behind the Work

Practice Before Theory

I found that learners were more successful when they could experience and practice a musical concept before being asked to connect it to formal notation and terminology.

Formative Feedback Loop

Assessment and adjustment were ongoing parts of the rehearsal process. Skills checks, observation, self-assessment, and real-time coaching helped me identify what learners needed next, adjust rehearsal priorities, and provide individualized feedback when a learner or section needed additional support.

Authentic Assessment

Concerts and performances served as real-world demonstrations of skill application and transfer.

Results & impact

What Changed

  • As foundational skills became more secure, rehearsals could move beyond note accuracy into interpretation, phrasing, historical context, and expression.
  • Learners demonstrated greater confidence and readiness as they moved toward live performance.
  • Post-performance reflection gave learners an opportunity to evaluate their own growth and identify what they could continue improving.
  • Ongoing assessment helped keep instruction responsive as learner needs and performance changed throughout the rehearsal cycle.

Design Reflection

This experience reinforced for me that successful performance depends on much more than final preparation. Learners need recurring feedback, accessible performance supports, opportunities to practice, and time to reflect on how their skills are developing. By the time they reached the concert, the goal was not for them to suddenly prove what they knew. The goal was for the performance to be the natural result of everything they had practiced, adjusted, and strengthened along the way.
